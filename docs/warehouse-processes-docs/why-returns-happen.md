# **Why Returns Happen (And How to Reduce Them)**

---

## 1. The Reality

Returns are not random.

→ **Every return is caused by something that went wrong earlier**

---

Most teams treat returns as:
→ a customer problem

But in reality:

→ returns are **operational signals**

---

## 2. The Core Idea

Every return can be traced back to one of these:

```text id="rt8x2m"
Receive → Stow → Pick → Pack → Ship
```

Something broke along this chain.

---

## 3. The Most Common Causes

---

### 1. Wrong Item Picked

What happened:

* similar item selected
* scanning skipped or bypassed

---

Where it started:
→ **Picking**

---

How to prevent:

* never pick without scanning
* always confirm with system

---

---

### 2. Damaged Item Shipped

What happened:

* item damaged before or during picking
* not detected

---

Where it started:
→ **Receiving or Picking**

---

How to prevent:

* log SKU-Gaps early
* never pick damaged items

---

---

### 3. Missing Item in Order

What happened:

* item not picked
* item not verified during packing

---

Where it started:
→ **Picking or Packing**

---

How to prevent:

* verify every item before sealing
* do not skip scanning

---

---

### 4. Item Not As Expected

What happened:

* wrong SKU received
* incorrect labeling
* mismatch in inventory

---

Where it started:
→ **Receiving**

---

How to prevent:

* verify during receiving
* log issues immediately

---

---

### 5. Poor Packaging / Condition

What happened:

* packaging damaged
* item not presentable

---

Where it started:
→ **Receiving, Storage, or Handling**

---

How to prevent:

* inspect during receiving
* maintain storage discipline

---

## 4. The Pattern (Important)

Returns are not isolated.

They follow patterns:

* same SKU
* same location
* same operator behavior
* same step in workflow

---

## Key Insight

→ **Returns show you where your system is breaking**

---

## 6. How To Reduce Returns (Systematically)

---

### Step 1 — Capture Every Issue

* log SKU-Gaps
* use Problem-Center
* never bypass the system

---

### Step 2 — Look for Patterns

* same item returning often
* same issue repeating
* same step failing

---

### Step 3 — Fix at the Source

Do NOT fix at return stage.

Fix at:

* receiving
* stowing
* picking
* packing

---

### Step 4 — Enforce System Behavior

* always scan
* always confirm
* never assume

---

## 7. What NOT To Do

Do NOT:

* treat returns as isolated
* restock without understanding cause
* ignore repeated issues
* fix symptoms instead of source

---

If you don’t fix the root:

→ the return will happen again

---

## 8. What Good Looks Like

* returns decrease over time
* same issues stop repeating
* inventory quality improves
* operations become predictable

---

## 9. The Goal

Not:
→ handling returns faster

But:

→ **having fewer returns to handle at all**

---

```text id="c2m9vp"
Return →
```

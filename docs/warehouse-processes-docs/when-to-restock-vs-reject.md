# **When to Restock vs Reject a Returned Item**

---

## 1. The Decision

Every returned item must go into one of three outcomes:

→ **Restock**
→ **Fix (if applicable)**
→ **Reject**

---

This document focuses on the core decision:

→ **Restock vs Reject**

---

## 2. The Principle

→ **Only restock items that are ready to be sold again—immediately.**

---

If not:

→ do NOT restock

---

## 3. What “Restock” Means

Restock means:

→ the item goes back into inventory
→ and can be picked for a new order **without any issues**

---

## Restock ONLY if all are true:

* product is undamaged
* packaging is intact or acceptable
* item is clean and presentable
* barcode is scannable
* all components are complete

---

If even one of these fails:

→ do NOT restock

---

## 4. What “Reject” Means

Reject means:

→ the item is removed from sellable inventory

---

This includes:

* physically damaged items
* missing parts
* unusable packaging
* hygiene issues (dirty, worn, used)
* barcode missing or unusable

---

Rejected items must NOT:

* return to shelves
* be picked again
* stay in active inventory

---

## 5. The Decision Flow (Simple)

```text id="b4x9zt"
Can this item be sold again immediately?

→ YES → Restock  
→ NO → Reject (or Fix if applicable)
```

---

## 6. What About “Fix”?

If the item is:

* slightly dirty
* needs relabeling
* minor packaging issue

---

Then:

→ do NOT restock immediately

Instead:

→ create a **Job To Be Done (SKU-Gap)**

After fixing:

→ re-evaluate for restock

---

## 7. What To Do (Step-by-Step)

---

### Step 1 — Inspect the Item

At the return station:

* check condition
* check packaging
* check completeness

---

### Step 2 — Scan the Item

* confirm identity in system

---

### Step 3 — Decide

Use the rule:

→ **Sellable now?**

---

### Step 4 — Take Action

* Restock → move to storage
* Reject → move to reject area
* Fix → assign task

---

### Step 5 — Record the Outcome

* log decision in system
* ensure traceability

---

## 8. What NOT To Do

Do NOT:

* restock “almost good” items
* ignore small defects
* assume someone else will check later
* skip logging

---

If you restock bad items:

→ they will be picked again
→ and returned again

---

## 9. Why This Matters

Poor decisions here cause:

* repeated returns
* increased refunds
* customer dissatisfaction

---

Correct decisions:

→ improve inventory quality
→ reduce downstream errors

---

## 10. Common Edge Cases

---

### Case 1 — Opened Packaging

→ If product is untouched and resellable → Restock
→ If not → Reject

---

### Case 2 — Slight Dirt or Dust

→ If cleanable → Fix
→ If not → Reject

---

### Case 3 — Barcode Damaged

→ Do NOT restock
→ Fix (relabel)

---

## 11. What Good Looks Like

* consistent decisions across team
* no questionable items in inventory
* clear separation of usable vs unusable

---

## 12. Quick Decision Rule (Operator Shortcut)

```text id="r7n3pl"
Sellable now?

→ Yes → Restock  
→ No → Reject or Fix
```
